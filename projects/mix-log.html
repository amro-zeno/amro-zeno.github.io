Material Mix Log

Operators measure raw material in buckets and caps. I built a log that turns those counts into kilograms and percentages, and then into the monthly consumption of every material in the pipe section.

My role
Designed and built it
Scope
9 pipe products · day and night shifts
Tools
Excel · schedules, summaries, recipe checks
Status
Used every month

01 · Problem

Nobody knew where the material went

Pipe products are made from a blend of virgin and recycled polyethylene with a colour masterbatch. Two-layer pipes have a different mix inside and outside. Without a daily record, there was no reliable figure for what each product actually used.

Units, not kilogramsMaterial was added by the bucket and the cap, so it was never recorded as a weight.
No written recipesThere was no single written standard to compare each day's mix against.
No consumption figuresPurchasing and management could not see how much of each material the section used.

02 · What I built

From a bucket count to a monthly report

  1. Daily logOne row per line, shift and layer: date, product, and the number of buckets or caps of each material.
  2. Units tableEach measuring unit has a set weight, so a count of buckets or caps converts to kilograms automatically.
  3. Mix schedulesSimple, readable formulas for each product: units × unit weight, and weight ÷ total × 100 for the percentage.
  4. Production scheduleRolls produced per day and night shift for every product, size and roll length.
  5. Monthly consumptionRolls × kg per roll × mix percentage, added up across every product that uses each material.
  6. Recipe checkA standard recipe for each product flags any mix that drifts more than 2 percentage points.

04 · Result

A monthly habit, and a half-year view

9pipe products tracked, including inside and outside layers.
H1 2026half-year report for management, built from the monthly logs.

The section can now answer how much of each material it used in a month, and which products consumed it. The half-year report combined output by product family, the top products and raw-material use into one view for management.
