Bottling Line Work Study

We studied the production lines of a PepsiCo bottling plant in Riyadh to find steps that add no value and to reduce the time lost to changeovers. Our plan brings a full changeover down from about 4 hours to 1.5 hours.

Site
Aljomaih Bottling Plants (PepsiCo bottler), Riyadh
Team
Amro Zeno, Osama Sunaallah · work shared equally
Methods
Flow process charts, value analysis, fishbone, SMED
Outcome
Changeover plan: 4 h → 1.5 h

01 · Context

The plant and the question

Aljomaih Bottling Plants is the official bottler of PepsiCo products in central Saudi Arabia. We visited its plant in Riyadh's New Industrial Area to see how a large-scale beverage operation runs, line by line.

Our question was simple: which activities on these lines do not add value, and how can we remove them or make them shorter? We used the methods from our course: flow process charts, value-added analysis and Lean tools.

03 · Findings

Where the time goes

During our visit one of the lines broke down. We built a fishbone diagram to set out the likely causes and their effect on line performance.

Changeovers stop the lineA flavour change takes about 1 hour per trial, a size change about 2 hours and a mould change about 1 hour.
Shared can lines lose setup timeMixing products between the KHS and Krones can lines cost about 15 hours a week in setup and changeover defects.
A practice worth keepingOn the NRB line, damaged bottles leave on a dedicated conveyor path, without anyone handling them.

05 · Recommendations

What we proposed

  1. Automated changeover systemThe most effective option: flavour, size and mould changes run from a pre-programmed sequence.
  2. SMED (Single-Minute Exchange of Dies)The simplest and lowest-cost option: separate internal and external tasks, standardise tools and run steps in parallel.
  3. Pre-flushing with clean-in-place (CIP)Clean the lines automatically before each changeover.
  4. Stronger preventive maintenanceCalibrate and inspect machines on a regular schedule to reduce breakdowns.
  5. Jidoka and KaizenStop the line automatically when a defect appears, and let operators fix problems and suggest improvements at the source.

We recommended a hybrid approach: automated changeover combined with SMED principles, which is fast, reliable and cost-effective. For the can lines, we suggested separating KHS and Krones production to remove the weekly setup losses.

06 · Reflection

What I took from it

The plant already runs at a high standard, so the problems were not obvious. The biggest gains were in time that nobody sees as waste: setup, changeovers and the people side, such as absenteeism and training.

The study taught me to measure before judging. A flow process chart turns a line that looks efficient into a list of minutes and metres you can question one by one.
